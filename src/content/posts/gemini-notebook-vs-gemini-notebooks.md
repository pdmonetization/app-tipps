Gemini Notebook and notebooks inside Gemini have similar names and connected workflows, but they are not interchangeable screens. Choose the starting point by what you need to do: examine a collection of sources, or work with that context inside the broader Gemini assistant.

This comparison is based on official documentation checked on October 6, 2026. It is not a hands-on benchmark. Our [existing Gemini Notebook review](/gemini-notebook-app-review/) gives the general verdict; this article focuses on choosing the right workspace.

## The practical distinction

The standalone Notebook app is the natural starting point when the documents themselves are central: reading, checking citations and making study outputs. Notebooks inside Gemini organize a continuing project alongside Gemini conversations and tools. Google describes [source continuity between the products](https://blog.google/intl/en-ie/products/notebooks-gemini-notebooklm/), but a shared source collection does not imply identical controls or availability.

| Task | Suggested starting point |
| --- | --- |
| Check what a document actually says | Gemini Notebook |
| Prepare a source-based audio summary | Gemini Notebook |
| Keep a project's assistant conversations together | Notebooks in Gemini |
| Use project sources while working with broader Gemini tools | Notebooks in Gemini |
| Turn one chapter into revision material | Start in Notebook, then choose the study tool available to your account |

These are editorial workflow suggestions, not exclusive product capabilities.

## A concrete example: comparing two courses

Imagine you have two course brochures and a note about your budget. Begin in Notebook and ask where the courses differ in fees, schedule and required equipment. Check the passages supporting the comparison.

Then, if the connection is available to your account, use that context inside Gemini to draft a message asking the course organizer about missing information. A good request is: “Draft questions about the timetable exceptions. Do not invent the answers or say I have enrolled.”

The division of work is useful because you first establish the evidence, then produce something from it. You can also do the project entirely in one interface if its controls meet your needs.

## Why a feature may be missing

Google's [current notebook help](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/16972047?hl=en) describes gradual availability, connected-app settings and dependencies on Keep Activity for certain notebook functions. It also distinguishes ordinary notebooks from study notebooks. At the time checked, that page says study notebooks are available on the web but not in the Gemini mobile app.

Some announcements describe expanding mobile access, while the help page retains narrower instructions. Treat that as a reason to check your actual account, not proof that every phone already supports the same workflow. School and work accounts may also depend on administrator settings.

## Avoid three confusing assumptions

**A missing button does not establish a paid requirement.** It may reflect the platform, account or rollout. Check the current help and in-product message before buying a subscription.

**A source in one workspace does not guarantee identical output elsewhere.** An assistant using additional tools may have a different context from a source-focused question. Ask it to identify whether a claim comes from your documents or another source.

**Turning on a setting is a personal choice.** Read what the account setting does before changing it just to unlock a workflow. A source-based task may be possible in the standalone product without that particular Gemini feature.
