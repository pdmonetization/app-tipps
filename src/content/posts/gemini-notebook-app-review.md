> **Reviewed and fact-checked:** 19 August 2026. Google renamed NotebookLM to Gemini Notebook in July 2026. Features, limits and availability can change after publication.

## Quick verdict

**Gemini Notebook is one of the most useful AI apps for understanding material you already trust.** Instead of treating the open web as its starting point, it builds answers, summaries and study tools around the sources you add to a notebook. The mobile app is especially good for asking questions while away from a computer and listening to Audio Overviews during a commute.

It is not a complete replacement for the web version. Preparing a large notebook, checking long citations and organizing many sources still feels more comfortable on a bigger screen. The app is also only as reliable as the material you give it: a confident answer grounded in a weak source is still a weak answer.

**App-Tipps rating: 4/5**

## Pros and cons

**Pros**

- Answers are grounded in the sources inside your notebook.
- Citations make it easier to check where a claim came from.
- Audio Overviews support background playback and offline listening.
- Useful for PDFs, websites, YouTube videos and pasted text.
- Notebooks can sync with Google's wider Gemini experience.

**Cons**

- Managing complex research is easier on desktop.
- AI summaries can flatten nuance or miss an important exception.
- Source and usage limits vary by account or subscription.
- The new Gemini branding can make the product relationship confusing.

## What is Gemini Notebook?

Gemini Notebook is the new name for NotebookLM, Google's source-based AI research tool. Google announced the rename on 16 July 2026 while keeping it as a standalone product. The change reflects a closer connection with the Gemini app and Google Search rather than a completely new service.

Each notebook acts as a focused workspace. You add the material you want to study, then ask questions about that material. The app can create summaries and other outputs without mixing every notebook into one general chat.

That separation is the main reason to use it. A notebook for a course, work project or buying decision can stay attached to its own evidence instead of becoming another long conversation with a general AI assistant.

## Adding and using sources

The Android listing says the app accepts long PDFs, websites, YouTube videos and text. This covers the most common research material, from a report saved on your phone to an article shared directly from a browser.

After sources are added, you can ask direct questions, request a comparison or ask the app to explain a difficult section. Answers include references back to the supplied material. Those citations do not guarantee that the interpretation is correct, but they reduce the time needed to verify it.

The best results come from a clearly defined notebook. Ten relevant sources about one subject are generally more useful than a pile of unrelated files. Descriptive source names also make citations easier to follow later.

## Citations are the app's strongest feature

General AI chatbots can provide a convincing answer without making it obvious which document supports each point. Gemini Notebook is designed around the opposite workflow: begin with a controlled source set and keep the answer connected to it.

This is particularly valuable for students, researchers and people reviewing internal documents. You can ask where two reports disagree, which source mentions a deadline or what evidence supports a conclusion, then open the citation instead of trusting the summary alone.

You should still check important claims. The app may misunderstand a table, overlook a qualification or combine two passages too aggressively. Citation checking is part of the workflow, not an optional task reserved for suspicious answers.

## Audio Overviews on mobile

Audio Overviews turn notebook material into a podcast-style discussion. On mobile, they are more than a novelty because the app supports background playback and offline access. You can generate an overview before travelling, download it and listen without keeping the screen active.

The format is helpful for reviewing a subject or discovering which parts deserve closer reading. It is less suitable for exact figures, legal language or any passage where wording matters. Treat the audio as a guided introduction, then return to the cited sources for precision.

The conversational delivery can also make a dry set of documents easier to approach. However, two natural-sounding voices do not make an interpretation authoritative. The evidence remains in the notebook, not in how confidently the hosts present it.

## Gemini integration: useful, but potentially confusing

Google says notebooks can work across Gemini Notebook and the Gemini app. In practice, that makes a notebook reusable context rather than a research project trapped in one application.

The benefit is continuity. You can organize sources in Gemini Notebook, then use the notebook from Gemini for a related task. The drawback is naming. "Gemini Notebook," notebooks inside Gemini and the separate Gemini app sound similar enough that a new user may not immediately know where a feature lives.

The simple distinction is this: use Gemini Notebook when the source collection and citations are central to the job; use the broader Gemini app when you need a general assistant that can draw on more tools and services.

## What the mobile app does less well

The small screen is the obvious compromise. Reading an answer and jumping through citations works well, but comparing several long passages is slower than placing them side by side on a computer.

Source preparation is another limitation. A notebook becomes much better when irrelevant material is removed and files are named clearly. That setup work can be done on a phone, but it is rarely the fastest place to do it.

Finally, the app can encourage passive learning. A summary, quiz or audio discussion feels productive, but understanding improves when you question the answer, inspect the evidence and write your own conclusion.

## Privacy and sensitive material

Before uploading private work, school or client documents, check the current account terms and your organization's policy. A useful research feature is not automatic permission to place confidential material in a third-party service.

For ordinary public sources, the practical risk is lower. For contracts, health records, unpublished research or internal company data, confirm what is allowed before creating the notebook.

## Who should use Gemini Notebook?

Gemini Notebook is best for:

- students working from lectures, readings and research papers;
- professionals comparing reports or project documents;
- writers who need a source-grounded briefing before drafting;
- commuters who want to review material through Audio Overviews;
- anyone frustrated by AI answers that are difficult to trace.

It is less suitable for people who only want a quick general chatbot, users who do most of their research without saved sources, or work that cannot be uploaded to a cloud service.

## Final recommendation

Gemini Notebook is worth installing if your work starts with documents rather than prompts. Its mobile app makes the core research loop portable, and citations give it an advantage over assistants that expect you to accept a polished answer without showing the path back to the evidence.

Use the phone app for capture, questions and listening. Use a larger screen for building complicated notebooks and checking detailed evidence. Most importantly, treat its output as a map of your sources—not a substitute for reading them.
