CapCut auto captions can turn spoken audio into editable subtitles, but generation is only the first pass. Names, numbers and short pauses still need attention. For a mobile workflow, check access on a short clip, correct the wording and timing, then style the captions for the final frame.

**Research scope:** official documentation checked on October 12, 2026. This is a researched guide, not a hands-on app test. The practice script below is an original example. Buttons and paid access can vary by version and account.

## Check access before captioning a whole project

CapCut's [mobile caption instructions](https://www.capcut.com/help/how-to-recognise-subtitles) describe the feature on iOS and Android. Feature support does not establish that it is free: its [plan comparison](https://www.capcut.com/resource/capcut-standard-vs-pro) places automatic caption generation among Pro tools, while some marketing pages advertise free captioning.

Inspect the badge and any allowance shown in your app. Make a short export attempt before committing to a long edit. A free download, preview or generation offer is not a promise of unrestricted export. For a subscription message, use our [paid-export checklist](/capcut-pay-before-export/).

## Generate captions on iPhone or Android

CapCut documents this mobile route: create a project, import the video, open **Captions**, select **Auto Captions**, confirm the spoken language and generate. Tap a subtitle box to change its text, timing or appearance. If your toolbar differs, look for the caption or subtitle tool; do not follow desktop sidebar instructions on a phone.

Finish major cuts before generating when possible. Captions attached to a section you later remove will need another check. If the project contains voiceover and camera audio, inspect the source options available rather than assuming the app will select the intended speaker.

## Correct the words before choosing a style

Record this original practice script at an ordinary speaking pace:

> Meet Maya at 6:15. Bring two cables, not three. The total is fifteen pounds. We are not cancelling the workshop.

The useful checks are “Maya,” the time, the cable count and both negatives. A transcription that drops “not” can reverse the instruction, even if every other word is right. Compare the captions with the recording, not your memory of what you meant to say.

| What to inspect | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Names and brands | Unfamiliar words can be replaced with more common ones |
| Prices and dates | A small numerical error changes the message |
| Negatives | Missing “not” can reverse a claim |
| Line breaks | A correct sentence can still be hard to read |

This example is a proposed exercise, not a claim about errors CapCut actually produced.

## Adjust timing and line breaks

Play the clip while reading the captions. Each segment should appear with the relevant speech and leave enough time to read it. If two thoughts have been combined into a crowded box, divide them using the controls your version provides, or shorten the display text without changing the meaning.

Avoid splitting a person's name or a phrase such as “not cancelling” across awkward line breaks. Keep the sentence understandable if a viewer glances away briefly. For instructional video, retain essential conditions rather than reducing everything to isolated keywords.

## Style for the final frame

Start with a readable font, strong contrast and restrained animation. Preview on the phone at normal size rather than zooming in on the editor. If there is an apply-to-all option, check a few other segments after using it; long sentences may wrap differently.

Choose the video shape first. Changing to portrait after positioning captions can push them outside the frame. Our [vertical-video guide](/capcut-vertical-video-without-cropping-subject/) explains fitting and cropping. Leave room for the destination app's interface; its overlay positions can change, so inspect a preview there before publishing.

## Verify the exported video

Watch the exported file outside CapCut. Check the first caption, a dense sentence and the ending. Confirm that text, sound and timing survived export. Captions visible in the editor are not a separate subtitle file: exporting an SRT or other timed-text file is a different feature and may have different platform restrictions.
