A missing CapCut project is not necessarily a deleted project. The editor can show local drafts, account-linked cloud projects and exported videos in different places. Start by identifying **what went missing**, then check where that version was stored. The most important early rule is **do not uninstall CapCut or clear app data while looking for the project**.

**Research scope:** official CapCut help pages checked on November 4, 2026. These are recovery checks, not a guarantee that deleted work can be restored. Interface labels and cloud features may vary by app version and region.

## First, identify what is missing

| What you can still find | What to check next |
| --- | --- |
| The final video in Photos/Gallery, but no editable timeline | Look for the original CapCut draft, not another exported copy |
| A project on your old phone but not your new one | Check whether it was uploaded to CapCut Space and which account is signed in |
| The project is listed, but clips are missing | Check the original media files and storage permissions before changing the timeline |
| Projects appeared to vanish after changing sign-in | Verify the original login method and cloud Space |
| Drafts disappeared after deleting app data or uninstalling | Look for a verified backup, but do not assume the app can recreate local files |

An exported MP4 is not the same thing as an editable project. Reimporting that video can help you make a new edit, but it does not restore the original separate text layers, clips and effects.

## Check CapCut's Projects view on the original phone

Open CapCut on the **same device** that contained the draft. From the home screen, inspect **Projects** or the current project library. CapCut's [help for editing previous projects](https://www.capcut.com/help/modify-history-task) describes mobile projects in the app's Projects view. Check the list before switching accounts, clearing anything or reinstalling.

If you can open the project, confirm that the clips, audio and captions are present. Before experimenting, consider duplicating it if your version offers that control. Save a finished export as an additional reference, but do not confuse an export with a backup of the editable draft.

If the project opens with missing media, check whether its source videos still exist in Photos or the phone's file manager. Moving or deleting source footage can leave an edit unable to find what it needs. Avoid deleting more footage just to free space while diagnosing the problem.

## Verify the exact account and sign-in method

CapCut's [cloud-access troubleshooting](https://www.capcut.com/help/cloud-entry) recommends checking the account and connection when cloud entries disappear. Someone who originally used a TikTok login and later signs in with an email or another provider may be looking at a different account, even if the visible address seems familiar.

Check your profile and the method used for the original project. If you manage more than one account, write down which one is currently open and inspect each legitimate account rather than trying to merge accounts blindly. This check is about **cloud-linked projects**; switching accounts cannot automatically recover a draft that existed only in local device storage.

## Check Space, not just the home screen

CapCut's [project history guidance](https://www.capcut.com/help/modify-history-task) explicitly says cross-device access depends on uploading projects to **Spaces**. Signing in alone does not prove that every old mobile draft was uploaded.

Open the Space or cloud area available in your version, check your projects and allow the list to sync over a stable network. If a cloud tab appears missing, CapCut's [Cloud entry help](https://www.capcut.com/help/cloud-entry) suggests checking the Space section and current account, updating the app and allowing synchronization.

A practical test: if a draft is visible on the original phone but not on the new one, inspect the original phone's upload or cloud status before changing devices. Do not wipe the original phone until you have opened the project successfully from the new location.

## What if it happened after a reinstall or cache cleaning?

CapCut's [connection troubleshooting](https://www.capcut.com/help/what-to-do-if-no-internet-connection-on-capcut) makes a crucial distinction between clearing app cache and clearing system **App Data**. Removing app data can permanently delete locally stored projects. CapCut also warns that uninstalling during troubleshooting may remove unsynced drafts.

Do not repeatedly uninstall, reinstall, use "Clear storage," or run phone-cleaner apps hoping to force a missing draft to reappear. Such steps can remove the very local data you are trying to preserve.

If the app was already reinstalled, check for a confirmed cloud copy from your original account. There may also be exports in Photos or your Files app. Those exports are useful, but a finished video is not proof that the old editable project can be restored.

CapCut's separate help pages are not perfectly consistent about deleted-project recovery: one [deletion article](https://www.capcut.com/help/delete-history-task) says deleted projects cannot be recovered, while its [project history article](https://www.capcut.com/help/modify-history-task) mentions a trash or recycle-bin check. If a Trash view is offered in *your* version, inspect it, but do not promise yourself a recovery option that may not exist for your device or type of deletion.

## Protect any draft you do recover

Use this sequence once you find an intact project:

1. **Verify it opens.** Scrub the timeline and confirm original clips, text and sound.
2. **Keep the source media.** A synced project may still depend on files that were not copied.
3. **Check cloud status.** Confirm an actual upload to Space rather than assuming sign-in was enough.
4. **Make a separate final export.** This preserves a playable reference, not the editable layers.
5. **Test before moving devices.** Open the uploaded project in the destination account/device before removing anything from the original.

For an original example, imagine a 45-second travel edit with eight phone clips, three captions and a soundtrack. Its final export may play perfectly, but if two source clips and the local project database are gone, the original editing timeline may not be reconstructable. That is why recovery begins with storage and account verification, not a new export attempt.

## When to contact CapCut support

If a project should be in cloud Space but does not appear, report the device, operating system, CapCut version, sign-in method (without sharing passwords), approximate date last seen, and whether it was visible locally or uploaded to Space. Include a screenshot of the missing-list view if safe. Avoid sharing private source videos unless support specifically needs and can securely accept them.
