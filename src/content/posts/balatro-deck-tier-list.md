The best deck in Balatro is not necessarily the one that can produce the biggest screenshot-worthy score. For a normal unseeded run, the better question is which deck gives you the most reliable route through Ante 8 when the shops are ordinary and the Jokers are not perfect.

That is the standard behind this Balatro deck tier list. We ranked all 15 decks for consistent wins, with extra weight given to early survival, economy and the freedom to change plans. A deck that only becomes brilliant after several lucky shops ranks below one that helps from the first Blind.

If you are still learning when to save money or replace a Joker, start with our [Balatro mobile beginner's guide](/balatro-mobile-beginners-guide/). The ranking below assumes you already understand Chips, Mult, Jokers, Blinds and basic shop decisions.

## Balatro deck tier list at a glance

| Tier | Decks | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| S | Yellow, Blue, Checkered | Reliable Ante 8 wins and learning the game |
| A | Ghost, Plasma, Anaglyph, Abandoned | Strong advantages with a clear strategy |
| B | Red, Zodiac, Magic, Nebula, Erratic | Useful bonuses with less consistency |
| C | Green, Painted | Playable, but their trade-offs become restrictive |
| D | Black | High ceiling, punishing early game |

This is an editorial ranking for completing Ante 8, not an Endless-mode high-score list. Stakes, unlocked Jokers and personal play style can move a deck by a tier. Balatro's official Steam listing confirms that the base game has [15 decks with distinct modifiers](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2379780/Balatro/), so every deck below is part of the standard game rather than a mod.

## S tier: the best Balatro decks for consistent wins

### 1. Yellow Deck

**Effect:** Start with an extra $10.

Yellow Deck is our best overall deck because money solves several early-run problems at once. The extra cash lets you buy a useful Joker without immediately destroying your interest, take a strong booster pack or survive an expensive first reroll.

That flexibility matters more than a narrow scoring bonus. If the first shop offers an economy Joker, you can invest. If it offers immediate Chips or Mult, you can stabilize. If it offers nothing useful, you can leave with enough money to earn interest instead of forcing a bad purchase.

Yellow Deck is particularly good for players learning that an unspent dollar can be more valuable than a weak card. Its advantage is less visible in the late game, but a healthy economy built in Antes 1 and 2 keeps paying for rerolls and upgrades long after the original $10 is gone.

### 2. Blue Deck

**Effect:** Gain one extra hand each round.

An extra hand is both a safety net and a source of money. It gives you another chance to find a scoring hand when a draw goes badly, and an unused hand adds to the cash earned after a win.

Blue Deck does not tell you which poker hand to play or which Joker to buy. That is a strength. It supports Pair, Two Pair, Flush, Straight and high-card builds without locking you into any of them. The effect also remains relevant on every Blind, unlike a one-time starting bonus.

For a first clear, Blue Deck is arguably the easiest choice. It forgives one more mistake per round while still rewarding efficient one-hand wins.

### 3. Checkered Deck

**Effect:** Start with 26 Hearts and 26 Spades.

Checkered Deck makes Flushes dramatically easier to draw from the opening round. That gives the run an immediate direction: find Flush-supporting scoring, improve Flush with Jupiter cards and use Tarot cards to strengthen one or both suits.

The deck ranks below Yellow and Blue because its consistency comes with a strategic warning. It is easy to spend too much on mediocre suit Jokers just because they match the deck. A strong general Chips Joker, scaling Mult Joker or economy engine can still be the correct purchase.

Checkered is excellent for a player who wants a simple plan, but Boss Blinds that debuff Hearts or Spades deserve extra attention. Keep a scoring option that does not collapse when one suit is disabled.

## A tier: powerful decks with a more specific plan

### 4. Ghost Deck

**Effect:** Spectral cards can appear in the shop, and the run starts with a Hex card.

Ghost Deck has one of the strongest ceilings because Hex can immediately make a good Joker Polychrome. The catch is that using Hex destroys the other Jokers you hold, so patience matters. Spending it on the first average Common Joker wastes the deck's best opening asset.

Wait for a Joker that can remain useful for most of the run, then build around it. Shop Spectral cards add more chances to create powerful editions or reshape the deck, but they also create tempting purchases that may not solve the next Blind. Ghost is strong when you can separate a spectacular effect from an actually useful one.

### 5. Plasma Deck

**Effect:** Balance Chips and Mult when scoring, while base Blind sizes are doubled.

Plasma Deck can turn a large pile of Chips into an enormous early score because it averages Chips and Mult before multiplying them. That makes efficient chip sources such as Stuntman, Bull, Blue Joker and scaling chip effects unusually valuable.

It is not S tier for general use because the doubled Blinds punish a weak transition. Flat Chips can carry the early game, but higher Antes usually require a plan that introduces meaningful X Mult without filling every Joker slot with small +Mult bonuses.

Plasma has enough unusual decisions to justify its own walkthrough. Our [Balatro Plasma Deck guide](/balatro-plasma-deck-guide/) explains the balancing formula, the best early Jokers and when to pivot toward X Mult.

### 6. Anaglyph Deck

**Effect:** Gain a Double Tag after defeating each Boss Blind.

Anaglyph Deck stores future value. Double Tags can multiply a useful skip reward, creating bursts of money, Negative Jokers, upgraded shop items or other strong bonuses later in the run.

The problem is that a Double Tag is not scoring power by itself. Hoarding several tags while your build struggles is a common way to lose with unused value. Play normal Blinds and take their shops unless a skip reward materially improves the current run. The best Anaglyph runs are selective, not addicted to skipping.

### 7. Abandoned Deck

**Effect:** Start with no face cards, leaving a 40-card deck.

Removing Jacks, Queens and Kings makes the starting deck thinner and improves the chance of finding repeated low and middle ranks. Four of a Kind and rank-focused strategies become easier to shape, and later card removal has a larger effect on draw consistency.

The trade-off is lower natural card Chips and no access to face-card synergies without adding cards back. Abandoned Deck rewards players who recognize what the first useful Jokers are asking for rather than forcing a preselected hand from round one.

## B tier: good decks that need the right run

### 8. Red Deck

**Effect:** Gain one extra discard each round.

Red Deck is dependable and easy to understand. The extra discard helps assemble Flushes, Straights and Full Houses, and it supports effects triggered by discarding. It ranks below Blue because an extra hand can also cycle cards, score points and generate end-of-round money. Red is still a sensible training deck for draw-dependent hands.

### 9. Zodiac Deck

**Effect:** Start with Tarot Merchant, Planet Merchant and Overstock.

Zodiac creates busy shops with more consumables and an additional item slot. That is useful once the run has a clear hand type and enough cash to exploit the selection. Early shops can still be awkward because seeing more cards does not mean you can afford them, and the increased consumable rate can crowd out Jokers. Build the economy before rerolling aggressively.

### 10. Magic Deck

**Effect:** Start with the Crystal Ball voucher and two copies of The Fool.

Magic Deck can duplicate an early Tarot or Planet play and begins with extra consumable capacity. A well-timed Hermit, strong suit conversion or key hand upgrade can create a fast start. The weakness is that its advantage depends on finding a consumable worth copying. Do not spend both Fools on trivial upgrades simply to clear the slots.

### 11. Nebula Deck

**Effect:** Start with the Telescope voucher but lose one consumable slot.

Telescope makes the most-played poker hand appear in every Celestial Pack, which supports a focused hand-level strategy. The reduced consumable space makes Tarot management clumsier, and a run that needs to pivot can feel trapped by its earlier Planet investment. Nebula is much stronger when you identify a reliable hand early and stop buying unrelated upgrades.

### 12. Erratic Deck

**Effect:** Start with 52 cards whose ranks and suits are randomized.

Erratic Deck can open with an extraordinary concentration of one rank or suit, but it can also produce an unhelpful mess. Inspect the full deck before the first Blind. If it contains eight copies of a rank, build around that evidence; do not play it like an ordinary 52-card deck.

Its best starts belong in A or even S tier, while its worst starts deserve C tier. B reflects that variance. Repeatedly restarting until the deck is perfect can make it look stronger than it is in normal play.

## C tier: restrictive trade-offs

### 13. Green Deck

**Effect:** Earn money from unused hands and discards, but earn no interest.

Green Deck pays immediately for efficient rounds, yet it removes the most reliable long-term economy mechanic. Difficult Blinds force you to spend hands and discards, reducing your income exactly when the run is under pressure. It can work with extra-hand, extra-discard and money-generating Jokers, but Yellow Deck produces a healthier economy with fewer conditions.

### 14. Painted Deck

**Effect:** Gain two hand-size slots but lose one Joker slot.

The larger hand makes Straights, Flushes and other draw-dependent combinations easier. Losing a Joker slot, however, removes 20% of the normal space for scoring, economy and utility. That cost becomes painful in later Antes, when a fifth Joker can be the difference between steady growth and a stalled build.

Painted Deck is useful for practicing difficult poker hands. It is less convincing when the sole goal is a consistent high-stakes win.

## D tier: the hardest deck to stabilize

### 15. Black Deck

**Effect:** Gain one Joker slot but lose one hand each round.

Black Deck offers a tempting late-game reward, but charges for it from the first Blind. One fewer hand means less room for error, less potential round income and fewer chances to dig through the deck. Meanwhile, the sixth Joker slot does nothing until you can find and afford six Jokers worth keeping.

The deck can produce excellent Endless runs once it is established. For ordinary unseeded Ante 8 wins—especially at higher stakes—the weak economy and fragile first Antes make it our clear last-place choice.

## Best Balatro deck for beginners

Choose **Blue Deck** if you want the most forgiving first clear. The extra hand keeps a bad draw from ending the run and works with any scoring plan.

Choose **Yellow Deck** if you understand basic scoring but keep wrecking your economy. The extra $10 creates better shop decisions and makes it easier to preserve interest.

Choose **Checkered Deck** if you want the clearest build direction. Flushes are immediately consistent, so you can focus on learning Joker synergy and Boss Blind preparation instead of deciding which hand to play.

## Best decks for higher stakes

Yellow and Blue remain reliable because money and extra hands solve problems without requiring a particular shop. Ghost has a higher ceiling when Hex lands on a run-defining Joker. Plasma can also be exceptional, but doubled Blind sizes make its midgame pivot more demanding.

At higher stakes, consistency matters more than a deck's theoretical maximum. Build enough score to survive first, keep an economy source when possible and treat a deck bonus as an advantage—not an instruction to reject every card outside one theme.

## Which Balatro deck should you try next?

For the shortest route to a first win, use Blue or Yellow. For a structured Flush run, use Checkered. For a run that changes how you evaluate scoring, unlock Plasma and learn why one large number can be better than balanced Chips and Mult.

No tier list can account for every Joker sequence. The useful test is whether a deck improves weak shops, survives awkward draws and leaves room to pivot. That is why simple advantages occupy the top of this ranking, while decks with dramatic late-game ceilings but punishing starts sit lower.
