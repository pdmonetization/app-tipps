> **Quick start:** Do not begin by transferring old Pokémon or spending every saved resource. First secure your login, inspect your collection, learn what changed and choose one goal for the week.

> **Research note:** This guide uses current official support documentation and company information. Menus and event bonuses can change between app versions, so confirm time-sensitive details in the in-game Today view and official news feed.

This Pokémon GO returning-player guide is designed to get an old account safely moving again without wasting Pokémon, Stardust or money.

Opening Pokémon GO after several years can feel like signing into somebody else's account. The map is familiar, but it now contains Routes, Power Spots, Max Battles, Party Play, more research tabs and a much longer progression path.

You do not need to master all of it immediately. This returning-player plan protects the account and its older collection first, then introduces current systems in an order that makes sense.

## Pokémon GO returning-player guide: the first 30 minutes

| Time | Action | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–5 minutes | Verify your login and account recovery options | Losing an old account is more costly than missing one event |
| 5–10 minutes | Review Pokémon and item storage without deleting anything | Older Pokémon may be useful, sentimental or eligible for special trade mechanics |
| 10–15 minutes | Check current research and the Today view | This shows what is active now instead of relying on an outdated guide |
| 15–20 minutes | Inspect nearby PokéStops, gyms, Routes and Power Spots | Your local map determines which activities are practical |
| 20–25 minutes | Choose one short-term goal | A single goal prevents the number of systems becoming overwhelming |
| 25–30 minutes | Adjust battery, notification and privacy settings | The defaults may not match how you want to play today |

## 1. Secure the account before doing anything else

An account created years ago may still depend on an old Google, Apple, Facebook or Pokémon Trainer Club login. Confirm that you can access the connected provider and that its recovery email and phone number are current.

If the game offers the option to connect an additional login provider, consider using one you control. Never share login credentials with another player, a raid service or a site promising free currency.

Also check whether you created a second account accidentally. Returning players sometimes sign in with a different provider, see a new level-one Trainer and assume their collection was deleted.

## 2. Do not mass-transfer old Pokémon

Storage pressure is usually the first irritation, but deleting hundreds of Pokémon immediately is risky.

Before transferring anything:

- search by year and review the oldest catches;
- keep Pokémon with personal or event significance;
- check IVs with the in-game appraisal tool;
- preserve useful raid attackers until you understand current alternatives;
- ask an experienced player about older trade value before cleaning aggressively;
- avoid spending Stardust on random upgrades simply because a Pokémon has high CP.

Create temporary tags such as `KEEP`, `CHECK` and `TRADE`. A rough sorting system is safer than trying to make permanent decisions during the first session.

## 3. Learn the four biggest additions

What counts as “new” depends on when you stopped. These are the systems most likely to surprise a returning player.

### Routes

Routes are predefined walking paths created by the game, official partners or other Trainers. They provide directed exploration and route-specific progress rather than leaving you to wander between map points.

Official support says Trainers can follow Routes after reaching level 7. Quality and availability depend on what has been created and approved nearby.

### Party Play

Party Play lets a small group complete shared challenges and receive bonuses while exploring together. A Party contains two to four nearby Trainers, and official support currently requires level 21 or higher.

This is useful for friends, couples and families already meeting in person. It is not remote matchmaking: participants need to remain physically close.

### Power Spots and Max Battles

Power Spots are locations associated with Max Pokémon. Players collect Max Particles and use them to enter Max Battles or train Max Moves.

Treat this as a separate progression track. You do not need to divert all Candy and resources into it immediately. Learn how the current boss, counters and group requirement work before investing.

### The level cap of 80

Trainer progression now extends to level 80. Official documentation says levels 70 to 80 require Level-Up Research tasks in addition to XP.

That is a long-term destination, not a returning-player emergency. Account level alone will not repair a weak raid team or teach current battle mechanics.

## 4. Choose one goal for your first week

Pokémon GO works better when you ignore most of the available objectives temporarily.

Good first-week goals include:

- complete one current research line;
- build a basic team for the current five-star raid boss;
- follow three local Routes;
- clear enough item storage to play comfortably;
- reconnect with one local community;
- earn a weekly walking reward;
- learn the basics of one GO Battle League format.

Do not choose “catch up with everyone.” A player who continued for several years has accumulated resources you cannot reproduce in a weekend, and you do not need to.

## 5. Rebuild useful teams before chasing perfect Pokémon

A functional team of six appropriate counters is more useful than one heavily upgraded favourite surrounded by weak options.

For raids:

1. Check the current boss and its weaknesses.
2. Search your storage by the relevant attack type.
3. Use the game's recommended team as a starting point, not an unquestionable answer.
4. Improve several usable attackers gradually.
5. Save rare resources until you understand whether a move or Pokémon is genuinely difficult to replace.

For PvP, league CP limits, moves and matchups matter more than simply selecting the highest IV percentage. Raid advice and GO Battle League advice are not interchangeable.

## 6. Find people before attempting difficult group content

Campfire is the official community companion for discovering activity, planning meetups and communicating with other players. Some functions appear inside Pokémon GO, while the standalone app provides the fuller experience.

Check:

- whether nearby gyms show regular raid activity;
- whether Campfire lists an established local group;
- event meeting points and organiser history;
- whether an event is suitable for new or returning players;
- what group size is recommended for the specific raid or Max Battle.

Meet in public places, protect personal location information and follow the game's safety guidance. A community feature is an organisational tool, not a guarantee that every stranger is trustworthy.

## 7. Fix the settings that affect daily play

Review the settings rather than assuming your old configuration survived unchanged.

### Battery and data

- Enable Battery Saver if it suits how you hold the phone while walking.
- Review Adventure Sync and the health-platform permissions it needs.
- Use Wi-Fi for large game-asset downloads when practical.
- Consider a power bank for long event days.
- Disable notifications that create urgency without helping your chosen goal.

### Privacy and location

- Review the phone-level location permission and background access.
- Check what information is visible through social features.
- Be careful when sharing catch cards or meeting locations.
- Do not play while driving or cross roads while concentrating on the map.

The game depends on real-world location. If that trade-off makes you uncomfortable, no settings guide can make it the right game for you.

## 8. Spend nothing during the first few sessions

The app is free to download, but storage, passes, event tickets and other conveniences can make spending feel immediately useful.

Wait until you can answer three questions:

1. Which limitation is actually stopping you from enjoying the game?
2. Can normal play or earned currency solve it?
3. Will the purchase still matter after the current event ends?

Storage can be a sensible long-term upgrade for an active collector. Buying resources before understanding the systems is much easier to regret.

## What to prioritise based on when you stopped

### If you stopped in 2016–2019

Focus on account recovery, appraisal, tagging, modern raids, Routes, Party Play, Mega Evolution and Max Battles. Expect the largest learning curve and avoid deleting old catches.

### If you stopped in 2020–2022

Review Routes, Party Play, the current community tools, Power Spots and the expanded level progression. Your understanding of raids and research will still provide a useful foundation.

### If you stopped in 2023–2025

Read the current season overview, inspect the Max Battle system and check how the level-cap changes affect you. Most core navigation should still feel familiar.

## Common returning-player mistakes

### Trying to complete every event

Limited-time activities overlap constantly. Choose events that support your current goal and ignore the rest.

### Powering up before checking moves

CP alone does not show whether a Pokémon has the right attacks or is useful for the activity you care about.

### Treating every rare item as urgent

Elite TMs, Rare Candy and other scarce resources should be used with a specific plan, not to tidy the bag.

### Assuming the local scene is dead

Players may coordinate through Campfire or meet only during scheduled events. Check before judging activity from a random weekday.

### Believing old guides without checking the date

Raid limits, event bonuses, shop contents, supported devices and progression rules change. Prefer official documentation for account, payment and safety questions.

## Is returning actually worth it?

Yes, if the original appeal was walking, collecting or meeting other players. The core idea remains distinctive and the game has enough active development to support long-term goals.

Returning is less attractive if you left because of location inequality, event pressure or optional spending. Those concerns have not disappeared, even though the game now offers more systems and social tools.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I restart Pokémon GO with a new account?

Usually no. An older account may contain valuable catches, medals, progress and memories. Restart only if you cannot recover it or deliberately want a clean challenge.

### What should I spend Stardust on first?

Nothing until you identify a concrete raid, PvP or collection goal. Stardust disappears quickly when upgrades are made without a plan.

### Do I need to learn Max Battles immediately?

No. Understand the basics and collect resources naturally, then invest when a useful boss or event gives you a reason.

### Can I return without a local community?

Yes for catching, research, Routes and much of collection play. Difficult group battles and trading will be more restrictive.

### How do I know what is happening this week?

Check the in-game Today view and the official Pokémon GO news feed. Event summaries from third parties can be helpful, but account and purchase decisions should be confirmed against official information.
