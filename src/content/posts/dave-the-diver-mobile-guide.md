Dave the Diver combines underwater exploration with running a sushi restaurant. The [official mobile listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.mintrocket.drmobile) describes a port of the original PC game, with a free opening portion and a paid unlock.

**Guide scope:** This overview explains the established game loop. We have not tested the global Android or iOS build, so it does not assess touch controls, phone performance or mobile balance.

## How diving and the restaurant connect

During the day, Dave explores the Blue Hole for fish, materials and quest items. At night, the catch supplies Bancho Sushi. Restaurant earnings then fund equipment for later dives.

Each trip therefore has two possible returns: progress toward a story objective and ingredients or materials for the next purchase. Chasing the deepest point on every dive is not automatically the most useful choice.

| Stage | What you do | What it supports next |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plan a dive | Choose a quest, ingredient or equipment goal | A reason to collect particular items |
| Explore | Gather fish and materials while managing oxygen and cargo | Restaurant stock and equipment resources |
| Return safely | Bring the haul back | Ingredients and progress you can actually use |
| Set the menu | Choose dishes supported by available stock | Sales during the evening |
| Run service | Get food and drinks to customers | Income for training and equipment |
| Reinvest | Address the problem that limited the previous session | A more productive next cycle |

For example, a dive that ends early because Dave is overloaded suggests a different next step from one that ends with empty cargo and no oxygen. The first calls for more selective collection or cargo capacity; the second calls for a shorter route, better survival or more air. Buying damage upgrades would not directly resolve either problem.

## What makes a dive risky?

Oxygen limits the time underwater and is also depleted by damage. Cargo competes for limited carrying capacity, while the Diving Suit determines safe depth. A route needs to leave room for the return journey as well as exploration.

The mobile store description specifically warns that running out of oxygen means leaving collected fish and items behind. Returning with a useful haul advances the next restaurant service; losing that haul breaks the cycle.

Changing Blue Hole layouts also make rigid directions less dependable. Learn to recognise depth, exits and landmarks rather than expecting every passage to stay in the same position.

## What happens at Bancho Sushi?

The restaurant turns diving into income. Stock determines what you can offer, while preparation and service determine how effectively you sell it.

A focused menu is easier to supply than a wide selection with only a few ingredients for each dish. As the restaurant develops, staff can take over jobs Dave would otherwise perform manually. The important question becomes which task is slowing service: preparation, delivering dishes, drinks, cleaning or wasabi.
