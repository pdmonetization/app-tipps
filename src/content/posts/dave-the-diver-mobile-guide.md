Dave the Diver comes to mobile with one of the strangest and most satisfying daily routines in gaming: catch fish in a mysterious Blue Hole during the day, then turn the catch into a profitable sushi menu at night. This Dave the Diver mobile guide explains the confirmed release model, what the full port contains and how its different systems fit together.

The important word is **full**. The official [Google Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.mintrocket.drmobile) and [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/dave-the-diver/id6759962259) describe the mobile edition as a full port of the original PC game. It is not a simplified restaurant app or a separate mobile spin-off.

You can download the opening portion without paying, decide whether the controls and performance suit your device, and then unlock the complete game through an in-app purchase. ## How the main gameplay loop works

Most ordinary days are divided into diving time and restaurant service. During a dive, Dave explores a Blue Hole whose layout and available creatures can change. Fish, materials and quest items compete for limited cargo space, while oxygen acts as both the dive timer and Dave's effective health.

Returning safely converts the dive into progress. Fish can become menu items, materials can support equipment improvements and quest objects open new systems or story chapters. Running out of oxygen is expensive because most of the collected haul is left behind.

At night, the focus shifts to Bancho Sushi. You choose dishes, decide how much stock to prepare, improve recipes, serve customers and eventually hire staff. Restaurant income pays for the equipment that makes deeper and more productive dives possible.

That creates the central cycle:

1. Catch useful fish and collect materials.
2. Return before oxygen or cargo becomes a problem.
3. Turn ingredients into a focused evening menu.
4. Earn money and improve equipment.
5. Reach deeper water and more valuable targets.

The two halves are not separate minigames. Waste ingredients at the restaurant and the next equipment upgrade takes longer. Ignore diving upgrades and the restaurant struggles to obtain better fish.

## What should you do first?

Follow the main objectives until the basic phone apps, weapon crafting and restaurant systems are introduced. Dave the Diver regularly presents a new side activity just as the existing routine becomes comfortable. Trying to optimize a system before its tutorial appears usually wastes time.

For permanent upgrades, early priority normally belongs to:

1. **Air Tank**, because more oxygen creates more time and a larger safety margin.
2. **Cargo Box**, because an overloaded diver moves slowly and may abandon valuable items.
3. **Diving Suit**, when the next objective requires greater depth.
4. **Harpoon Gun**, when ordinary catches are resisting the current equipment.

Do not upgrade every category equally. Buy the improvement that removes the current bottleneck. If oxygen repeatedly forces an early return, a stronger weapon will not fix the problem. ## Diving without losing the whole haul

The best early dive is not the one that reaches the greatest depth. It is the one that returns with the ingredients and materials needed for the next specific upgrade or menu.

Before entering the water, choose one main objective: story progress, a particular ingredient, weapon materials or restaurant stock. Collect opportunistic fish along the route, but leave enough cargo capacity for the target.

Watch three limits:

- **Oxygen:** damage and time underwater both reduce the same survival resource.
- **Cargo:** exceeding the comfortable limit slows movement and makes emergencies harder.
- **Depth:** going below the suit's rating quickly creates avoidable danger.

Oxygen capsules and escape devices are safety tools, not an excuse to remain underwater indefinitely. When the inventory contains rare quest materials or a strong restaurant haul, returning early is often the profitable decision.

## Catching fish for quality, not just quantity

Early equipment encourages the simple approach of using the harpoon or a weapon until a fish is captured. That works for basic stock, but the game later rewards bringing fish back in better condition.

Non-lethal tools such as nets and tranquilizing equipment become important because capture quality affects ingredient value and collection progress. The right method depends on the size and behavior of the target; there is no single tool that cleanly captures everything.

For a new save, do not delay progress because every catch is not perfect. Secure reliable ingredients first. Quality optimization becomes more valuable after the restaurant, weapon shop and equipment economy are stable.

## Running Bancho Sushi efficiently

A crowded menu looks impressive but can create waste. Select a small number of dishes supported by the current catch, improve recipes that can be supplied repeatedly and keep an eye on both price and available portions.

The **Auto Supply** option is useful because it adds ingredients as a dish sells rather than preparing the entire stock at once. It reduces the chance that unused portions disappear at the end of service. It does not create ingredients, so the kitchen can still run out when demand exceeds inventory.

Early restaurant priorities are straightforward:

- Use dependable ingredients instead of one rare dish with only a single portion.
- Enhance recipes that can be replenished on normal dives.
- Serve customers quickly enough that they do not leave unhappy.
- Recruit and train staff when Dave can no longer cover every service task.
- Preserve rare ingredients until their best recipe or objective is understood.

The restaurant is the game's main economic engine. A clean, repeatable menu often earns more useful progress than chasing the most exotic fish available.

## Weapons and equipment explained

The Harpoon Gun is Dave's standard capture tool, while weapon cases found underwater offer additional ways to deal with aggressive creatures. Duff's weapon systems eventually allow permanent weapon construction and upgrades using collected materials.

Separate two jobs when choosing equipment:

- **Defence and bosses:** damage, range and safe attack windows matter.
- **Live capture:** nets, tranquilizing effects and appropriate recovery equipment matter.

A high-damage weapon can solve a dangerous encounter while ruining the quality of a fish you wanted for the restaurant. Carry equipment for the actual purpose of the dive rather than automatically selecting the weapon with the largest damage number.

## Is Dave the Diver a roguelike on mobile?

The Blue Hole includes changing layouts and run-based risk, but Dave the Diver is not a traditional start-over-from-zero roguelike. Equipment, restaurant development, story progress, recipes, staff and many upgrades persist between dives.

Failure hurts the current haul rather than deleting the whole save. That makes the game suitable for mobile sessions: one dive or one restaurant service has a natural stopping point, while the larger progression continues across many days.

The catch is that a supposedly quick session can easily grow. A dive creates ingredients for the restaurant, service creates money for an upgrade, and the upgrade makes one more dive tempting.
