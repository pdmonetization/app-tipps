To put a landscape clip in a vertical video without cutting off important content, fit the whole image inside a portrait canvas and accept space above and below it. Filling the frame uses cropping unless you distort the image or create additional content. That is a composition choice, not a resolution setting.

**Research scope:** official documentation checked on October 12, 2026. The geometry and practice exercise are original editorial examples. This is not a hands-on CapCut test; tool names and automated options vary.

## Set the canvas before positioning text

Create a mobile project and import the clip. Find the project aspect-ratio or format control and choose 9:16 for a portrait canvas where that is your target. TikTok's [CapCut feature guide](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/about-capcut?lang=en) documents Format as the aspect-ratio control. Labels can change across app versions.

Aspect ratio describes shape. Resolution describes the number of pixels in that shape: 1080 × 1920 is one 9:16 example. Increasing pixels does not restore a person cropped out of the composition.

## Choose fit or fill deliberately

| Approach | Benefit | Trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fit the whole landscape image | Keeps both sides visible | Leaves space in the vertical canvas |
| Scale up to fill portrait | Uses the whole frame | Removes much of the left and right content |
| Reposition the crop | Keeps one moving subject visible | May lose other people or objects |
| Use a background behind the fitted image | Uses the spare canvas area | Background must not distract from the foreground |

Select the clip and adjust scale and position in the preview. To retain the whole scene, scale it down until both horizontal edges are visible. Use the background controls your version provides, or a plain background if that meets the purpose.

## Why the crop can be so severe

Consider a 1920 × 1080 landscape clip. A full-height 9:16 crop is approximately 607.5 pixels wide: 1080 × 9 ÷ 16. That is about 31.6% of the original width. Roughly two-thirds of the horizontal view falls outside that portrait crop.

The opposite choice is to fit the 1920-pixel width inside a 1080-pixel-wide portrait canvas. The visible clip becomes 1080 × 607.5, leaving substantial space vertically. These calculations explain the trade-off; they are not assertions about CapCut's internal rounding.

## Try a two-subject exercise

Use a landscape clip containing one person near each edge, or film two stationary objects on a table. Fit the clip and confirm both remain visible. Then fill the portrait frame and observe which object leaves the frame.

Choose based on the message. For an interview where both people matter, a smaller fitted picture may be clearer. For a close-up of one person, a crop may be useful. Stretching the image merely to avoid empty space changes proportions and is usually a poor substitute for that choice.

This is a suggested exercise, not a demonstration we recorded.

## Follow movement through the whole clip

Check the beginning, middle and end, then play the section continuously. A crop that works on the first frame can lose the subject when they move sideways. Where keyframes are available, adjust position at relevant points and inspect the movement between them.

If you use an automatic reframing tool offered by your account, review its result rather than assuming it knows which subject matters. Such tools may be paid or unavailable on your version. Manual fit-and-position work does not require assuming a particular AI feature exists.

## Place captions after framing

Keep essential text inside the visible canvas and away from the subject's face or the action you are explaining. Destination apps add interface overlays, so inspect their preview rather than relying on a fixed universal safe-area measurement.

Our [caption guide](/capcut-auto-captions-mobile/) covers text corrections and styling. If you change the canvas later, recheck every caption and title; a position that was comfortable in landscape can be clipped in portrait.

## Inspect the export outside the editor

Export a short example using settings your account supports, then open it in the phone's video player. Confirm that the shape, background, subject and captions are correct. Keep the project until the final file has been reviewed.
