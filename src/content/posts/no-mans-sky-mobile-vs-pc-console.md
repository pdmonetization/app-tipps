**No Man's Sky mobile vs PC** is a feature decision as well as a screen-size decision. The announced iPhone/iPad port brings the exploration game to mobile, but has no multiplayer support, restricts Planetary Settlements by hardware, and has not established iOS cross-save support in the official sources checked here. Those points matter more than simply calling it “the full game.”

Checked October 19, 2026, before the public mobile release. This comparison uses published platform information; it does not rank frame rates from invented tests. Use our [launch and price guide](/no-mans-sky-ios-release-date/) for the purchase model and [compatibility guide](/no-mans-sky-iphone-compatibility/) for exact-device checks.

## No Man's Sky mobile vs PC: the decision table

Start with the activity you want to do. A portable solo adventure, a shared session with friends and continuing an established PC save are three different use cases. An edition can suit one without meeting all three.

| Your priority | What the iOS announcement establishes | Decision before buying |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Exploring on a phone or tablet | A native mobile version with redesigned touch controls | Check eligibility and try the opening section |
| Playing together with friends | Multiplayer is not supported | Choose a platform combination that explicitly supports your group |
| Managing Planetary Settlements | A separate iPhone 15 Pro-and-above condition | Confirm your exact device's feature access |
| Continuing an existing PC or console save | iOS support is not confirmed by the checked cross-save sources | Do not buy on an assumed transfer promise |
| Using a controller | Controller support is announced | Verify the controller you already own and available controls |
| Playing on a TV through the mobile device | Casting is announced | Assess your own setup after release; casting does not add game features |

The table is an editorial decision aid built from the confirmed facts and their limits. It is not a claim that every PC or console edition has identical features either. In particular, different Switch generations have their own history; “console” should not be treated as one interchangeable specification.

## What comes across to the mobile edition?

[Hello Games' iOS announcement](https://www.nomanssky.com/2026/10/no-mans-sky-ios/) describes an extensive port incorporating the game's accumulated updates, including the recent Cosmos release and earlier Voyagers content. The intention is the established exploration experience, rather than a separate small game merely sharing its name.

That breadth should not erase the exceptions in the same announcement. When a publisher describes a full universe or years of content, read the platform-specific conditions before applying the phrase to a feature you care about. A large amount of solo content cannot substitute for an unsupported multiplayer session.

For a newcomer, it is more useful to identify the core activity that attracts you—discovery, building, trading or exploration—than to read a long list of update names. For an existing player, name the exact system you want to carry over. That turns a vague comparison into a checkable buying question.

## Multiplayer and cross-play are the clearest restriction

The developer explicitly says multiplayer is unsupported on iOS. Consequently, this launch should not be bought as a way for an iPhone player to join a PC or console friend in a multiplayer session. A shared discovery universe, an online service or a store's Game Center label is not evidence of co-op support.

Keep three concepts separate: **multiplayer** means players sharing a session; **cross-play** means a supported multiplayer connection between platforms; **cross-save** means moving saved progress. One being present elsewhere in the game does not prove the other two exist on iPhone.

For example, if two friends intend to buy copies specifically to explore together, the mobile announcement already answers the decisive question. There is little value in choosing between phone storage capacities before resolving that missing feature. Pick an explicitly supported platform pairing, then check its own multiplayer requirements.

## Planetary Settlements have a separate device condition

It would also be inaccurate to say settlements are absent from every iPhone. Hello Games names **iPhone 15 Pro and above** for that feature. The main game has a broader announced device range, so installation eligibility alone cannot settle the question.

That wording leaves a model-by-model question for some later non-Pro devices and iPads. We are not inventing a chip-tier table to fill it. If managing settlements is central to your existing routine, obtain confirmation for your exact hardware rather than assuming every newer release qualifies.

Our [compatibility article](/no-mans-sky-iphone-compatibility/) explains the base-game and feature checks separately. For a buyer, this is the difference between “I can install it” and “this copy lets me do what I am buying it for.”

## Cross-save on iPhone remains a question to verify

The official [cross-save service](https://cloud.nomanssky.com/cross-save) describes support across PC, PlayStation, Xbox, Switch and Mac. The [original cross-save announcement](https://www.nomanssky.com/2024/11/cross-save-ps5-pro-normandy/) establishes that progress transfer is a real part of the wider game. Neither fact alone establishes iOS participation.

The checked iOS announcement does not confirm the mobile account-linking route. The careful status is therefore **unconfirmed in these sources**, rather than either “definitely supported” or “permanently impossible.” A platform joining the same franchise does not automatically join every existing account service.

Consider a player with a long-established desktop save who wants to carry it on a commute. Their purchase depends on transfer support, not merely on whether the phone can render a planet. Wait for explicit iOS documentation or the relevant controls in the released build before planning around that workflow.

If support becomes available, verify the source and destination accounts and understand conflict handling before choosing between saves. Keep the existing local save while checking a transfer. An account-linking experiment should not begin by deleting the progress it is supposed to preserve.

## Touch, controller and larger-screen choices

The announced input options include redesigned touch controls and controller support, plus mouse and keyboard on iPad. These are platform features to assess; they do not establish that a particular accessory has been tested by us with the mobile game.

Start with what you own. A phone held in the hands, a tablet on a stand and a desktop monitor require different posture and viewing distance. Check text and inventory comfort alongside movement controls. Apple's [wireless controller guide](https://support.apple.com/en-us/111099) explains pairing and notes that specific features vary by controller and app.

Casting to a TV changes where you see the picture. It does not turn the device into a console edition, remove hardware feature restrictions or enable multiplayer. The portable device still needs to run the game and provide a suitable input route.

## Which edition fits three common situations?

**You are new and want solo exploration on a device you own.** The announced mobile version is worth evaluating through its free beginning. Check device eligibility first, then make the purchase decision from your own experience of the released version rather than a promised performance number.

**You already play with a regular group.** Resolve supported multiplayer platforms first. Do not replace an established co-op setup with an iPhone purchase expecting an unsupported session to become available through Game Center, casting or a controller.

**You want a portable companion to an established save.** Treat confirmed progress transfer as a requirement, not an optional detail. A separate fresh mobile adventure may still appeal, but that is a different purchase purpose from continuing the same save on the train.

## What to check when the public build arrives

The release-day questions are specific: does your device install the app, are the intended controls comfortable, is your desired feature present, and does any newly documented save workflow work with your accounts? Performance testing should record the device, OS, game version and settings before reporting results.
