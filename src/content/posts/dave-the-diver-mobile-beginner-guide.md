The safest opening in Dave the Diver is built on small successful dives, not heroic attempts to reach the bottom immediately. This Dave the Diver mobile beginner guide gives the first week a clear order: follow the story, improve the limiting piece of equipment, bring useful fish back alive and keep Bancho Sushi's menu under control.

The Android and iOS edition is officially described as a full port on the [Google Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.mintrocket.drmobile), so the core progression principles from the original game apply. Mobile controls and performance may change how comfortable a dive feels, but they do not change the relationship between oxygen, cargo, ingredients and restaurant income.

If you are still waiting to install it, check the [Dave the Diver mobile release date and download guide](/dave-the-diver-mobile-release-date/). The global release is scheduled for September 17, 2026.

## Dave the Diver mobile beginner guide: first priorities

Use this order when the game begins opening its systems:

1. Complete the current main objective and its tutorial.
2. Upgrade oxygen when dives end because the Air Tank is empty.
3. Upgrade cargo when weight repeatedly slows the return trip.
4. Improve the Diving Suit only when the next useful area requires it.
5. Build a small restaurant menu from ingredients you can replace.
6. Save before buying upgrades that do not solve a current problem.
7. Return to the boat with a good haul instead of gambling everything on one more room.

Dave the Diver introduces features gradually. Let the story unlock the phone apps, weapon systems and restaurant options before trying to perfect them.

## Treat oxygen as health and time

Oxygen decreases naturally while Dave is underwater and also absorbs damage. A careless encounter therefore costs both health and exploration time.

Keep enough oxygen for the return route. A tank showing half capacity does not mean half the dive remains if the next objective is deeper, enemies block the path or cargo has reduced movement speed.

Use oxygen capsules when they support a planned route. Do not collect one at full capacity simply because it is nearby if you expect to return through the area. A saved capsule can turn the exit into a safe path.

When oxygen reaches zero, most of the current catch and materials are lost. One extra fish or chest is rarely worth risking everything already collected.

## Do not ignore cargo weight

Every fish and material adds weight. Crossing the comfortable cargo limit slows Dave, which increases oxygen use on the way back and makes attacks harder to avoid.

Before a dive, decide what the trip is for. If the goal is a story item or crafting material, reserve capacity instead of filling the inventory with common fish near the surface.

When the bag becomes heavy:

- discard low-value common items before rare materials;
- stop collecting duplicates without a menu or upgrade purpose;
- use the nearest safe exit rather than repeating the entire route;
- upgrade the Cargo Box if weight is ending most dives before oxygen does.

Efficient inventory is more important than maximum inventory. Returning with the right materials creates progress; returning slowly with random items creates clutter.

## Upgrade the current bottleneck

iDiver upgrades can consume restaurant income quickly. Buying the cheapest available improvement each time spreads money across stats that may not help the next objective.

Use this decision rule:

| Problem ending the dive | Best first response |
| --- | --- |
| Oxygen runs out | Improve the Air Tank and shorten the route |
| Dave becomes too slow | Improve the Cargo Box and collect selectively |
| Depth causes damage | Improve the Diving Suit |
| Common fish resist capture | Improve the Harpoon Gun |
| Aggressive creatures stop progress | Prepare an appropriate weapon through Duff |

Air and cargo are strong general investments because they improve almost every dive. The suit is essential when needed but provides little benefit if the current route stays within its depth limit.

## Follow objectives before farming heavily

Early story missions unlock systems that make farming more efficient. Spending several dives building a giant stock of low-value fish can delay access to better equipment, staff, recipes and capture tools.

Complete the next sensible objective unless the equipment clearly cannot handle it. Gather useful ingredients and materials along the route, but do not turn the opening hours into an unrestricted grind.

Side quests are still valuable. The distinction is between completing a nearby request and repeating shallow dives without a progression target.

## Learn what changes between dives

The Blue Hole is designed to change. A route, fish location or useful object found on one dive may not appear in exactly the same place next time.

Build navigation around landmarks and depth rather than memorizing every turn. Note the direction of exits, oxygen supplies and dangerous zones as you descend. When a quest marker points deep into the map, leave spare oxygen for exploration around it.

This changing structure also means a failed search is not always a skill problem. Return, improve equipment and try the objective on another layout instead of spending the entire oxygen tank forcing one route.

## Use weapons for a specific job

The standard Harpoon Gun is useful for ordinary fishing, while underwater weapon cases provide temporary tools during a dive. Duff's systems later allow permanent weapons and upgrades.

Do not evaluate every weapon only by damage. Ask what the next dive requires:

- A strong firearm can protect a story route or handle a boss.
- A long-range option can make aggressive fish safer.
- Nets and tranquilizing tools support higher-quality live capture.
- A weapon that kills quickly may be poor for valuable restaurant targets.

Collect weapon blueprints and materials naturally while progressing. Avoid exhausting restaurant income on multiple branches before knowing which problem each branch solves.

## Understand fish quality

How a fish is captured affects the quality of the ingredient returned to the restaurant. Lethal methods are accessible early, but non-lethal capture becomes increasingly important.

Do not obsess over perfect quality on the first few dives. The opening needs dependable stock and story progression. Once better tools are available, target valuable species with a suitable net, tranquilizer and recovery method.

Think of capture quality as a second optimization layer:

1. First learn to find and return with the fish safely.
2. Then learn the correct non-lethal tool for its size.
3. Finally build repeatable high-quality supply for profitable dishes.

This prevents an early player from wasting oxygen trying to perfect a capture without the equipment required to do it cleanly.

## Keep the Bancho Sushi menu small

At the restaurant, more dishes do not automatically mean more profit. Every prepared item consumes ingredients, and unsold prepared portions can become waste.

Use a short menu based on the catch you can supply. Compare the selling price, available servings and whether the ingredient is needed for a quest or stronger recipe. Enhance dependable dishes instead of distributing resources across everything.

The **Auto Supply** option helps control waste by adding ingredients as sales occur. Use it for dishes with enough stock, but remember that it can continue consuming ingredients as customers order.

A practical early menu normally includes:

- one reliable dish with several portions;
- one stronger dish using a more valuable catch;
- enough total servings for expected customers;
- no rare ingredient that should be preserved for an objective.

Restaurant service is an operations problem. A small menu that stays available is better than a long menu that runs out unpredictably.

## Hire and train staff with a purpose

Dave can cover early restaurant tasks, but service becomes harder as customer numbers and drink responsibilities grow. Staff allow the restaurant to scale without making every evening a manual sprint.

Read the role and skills before investing. A server who can handle a recurring service task may improve the whole evening more than a staff member with slightly higher raw numbers. Kitchen staff matter when dish preparation becomes the bottleneck.

Do not train every recruit evenly. Choose the people who solve the present service weakness and train toward meaningful skill unlocks. Dispatch can later turn unused staff into a source of ingredients, giving specialised employees value outside the active restaurant lineup.

## Avoid these early mistakes

- **Diving until oxygen is almost empty.** The return route is part of the dive.
- **Collecting everything.** Weight can turn a safe exit into a loss.
- **Upgrading every stat equally.** Remove the problem that is actually stopping progress.
- **Killing every valuable fish.** Better capture tools can improve ingredient quality later.
- **Preparing too many dishes.** Unsold food wastes stock.
- **Ignoring Auto Supply.** Controlled replenishment is easier than guessing every portion.
- **Spending all restaurant income immediately.** The next story step may reveal a more useful upgrade.
- **Assuming the map is fixed.** Routes and opportunities can change between dives.
- **Forcing side content before its tutorial.** Many systems become clearer when introduced naturally.

## A simple first-session route

For the first mobile session, keep the plan deliberately modest:

1. Complete the tutorial objective.
2. Practice movement and aiming in shallow water.
3. Catch several common fish without exceeding the comfortable weight limit.
4. Open useful crates but do not chase every object on the map.
5. Return with a clear oxygen reserve.
6. Build a small evening menu.
7. Observe whether serving, kitchen speed or ingredient supply causes the first restaurant problem.
8. Spend the resulting income on the upgrade that addresses the next dive.

This route also tests the mobile port properly. A few minutes on the boat cannot reveal whether touch controls remain comfortable during combat or whether the phone heats during a complete dive and restaurant cycle.

## Should mobile players use a controller?

Official controller support is not listed before launch, so touch should be treated as the default. If the shipping build supports a gamepad, it may help during aiming and fast movement, but the feature must be verified rather than assumed from PC and console versions.

## The best beginner mindset

Dave the Diver rewards preparation more than speed. A safe return with the material for one upgrade and enough fish for a focused menu is a successful day.

The game becomes larger as farms, staff systems, special dives and new regions appear. Do not try to manage the late game during the opening week. Follow the story, improve one bottleneck at a time and let the dive-and-restaurant cycle teach you what the next investment should be.
