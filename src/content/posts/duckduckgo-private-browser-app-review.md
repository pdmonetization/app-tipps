## Quick verdict

DuckDuckGo’s free browser is a sensible privacy upgrade for people who want useful protections enabled by default. It blocks many known third-party trackers and adds tools beyond private search, but it does not make a user anonymous and should not be confused with a full VPN.

## What the app does in 2026

The mobile browser combines DuckDuckGo Search with tracker-loading protection, cookie controls and stronger HTTPS use where available. Optional Email Protection creates @duck.com forwarding addresses and removes several kinds of hidden email trackers. On Android, App Tracking Protection can block many tracking requests made by other apps; DuckDuckGo says this happens locally on the device.

## Main features

- Known third-party tracker blocking in the browser
- Private search without building a personal search profile
- Free Email Protection and disposable private addresses
- Android App Tracking Protection for many other apps
- A simple Fire button for clearing browsing data

## Pricing and fees

The browser, search and the privacy features covered here are free. DuckDuckGo also offers optional paid services in some markets, but they are not required for the browser’s core protections.

## Limitations and risks

Tracker blocking cannot stop every form of fingerprinting, first-party tracking, account-based profiling or information you knowingly submit. Android App Tracking Protection uses the operating system’s local VPN slot but is not a conventional VPN and does not hide all traffic or location. Some sites may break until protection is adjusted.

## Who should use it?

Best for users who want a low-effort privacy improvement without configuring many extensions. People who need corporate policy controls, advanced extension ecosystems or traffic tunnelling should compare other browsers and a reputable VPN separately.
